## 43 sites now running DSF 2.1.0 in production
In the second quarter of 2026, the coordinated rollout of [DSF 2.1.0](/operations/v2.1.0/release-notes.html) was successfully completed. All 43 participating sites, 38 German university hospitals and five additional research sites within the Medical Informatics Initiative (MII) and the Network University Medicine (NUM) are now running DSF 2.x in production. Most sites also maintain a separate DSF test environment. This marks a major milestone for the DSF community: for the first time, all participating production sites share a common DSF 2.x technical foundation.

DSF 1.9.0 has been completely phased out, marking an important breakthrough for the DSF community. The participating sites form a distributed network in which each organization operates its own FHIR server and Business Process Engine. Establishing DSF 2.1.x as a common technical baseline across the network creates the conditions for new and updated process plugins to be deployed consistently, without version incompatibilities between participating sites.

Following the successful rollout, the [DSF v2.1.1](/operations/v2.1.1/release-notes.html) maintenance release was published in August 2026. The latest DSF versions, v2.1.2 and v2.1.3, were released last week and this week respectively, following the discovery of security vulnerabilities related to [Java4Shell](https://calif.io/java4shell). Check out the latest changes: [DSF v2.1.3](/operations/latest/release-notes.html).

## A new foundation for process development
DSF 2.x introduced Process Plugin API v2, providing developers with cleaner abstractions, expanded metadata and new services. These include support for FHIR validation, encryption and compression, improved logging and reusable connections to local FHIR servers with password, mutual TLS and OpenID Connect authentication. At the same time, a compatibility layer enables existing API v1 process plugins to continue running. This avoided the need for a disruptive, simultaneous migration of every plugin and allowed the infrastructure and its processes to evolve independently. [Learn more about the architecture introduced with DSF 2.x.](/operations/v2.0.0/release-notes.html)

With DSF 2.1.x now established across all participating production sites, process developers have a consistent foundation for migrating existing plugins to API v2 and developing new processes. API v2 migrations are already underway for several widely used process plugins, including Ping-Pong, MII Report, MII Data Sharing, MII Feasibility and MII Data Transfer. The first migrated plugins are expected to be released and rolled out soon.

## Open-source collaboration with Operaton
Another major change in DSF 2.x is the transition of the Business Process Engine from Camunda 7 to [Operaton](https://operaton.org/). Operaton was created as a free and open-source continuation of the Camunda 7 Community Edition codebase following the end of its official support. Licensed under Apache 2.0, it is developed openly and maintained by an independent community rather than controlled by a single commercial organization. 

The collaboration goes beyond replacing one technical dependency with another. The DSF team followed Operaton’s development from an early stage, tested milestone releases and validated the engine against the requirements of a complex distributed healthcare infrastructure. Feedback from this work helped identify integration issues before the production rollout. In turn, Operaton gives the DSF community a transparent and sustainable BPMN 2.0 engine whose future can be shaped through open collaboration. 

Running Operaton provides a strong real-world demonstration of the engine’s ability to support secure, distributed workflows at national scale. Overall, the rollout of DSF 2.1.0 and the adoption of Operaton mark a new era for the DSF community and further reinforces our commitment to open-source principles. [Read more about our collaboration](2026-11-16-operaton-powers-dsf.md)

## Ready for the next generation of use cases

DSF 2.1.x further strengthens the foundation with security fixes, performance improvements and greater operational stability. Among other changes, these releases have improved the execution of allow-list operations and other FHIR bundle transactions.

The completed migration provides a reliable platform for existing and future use cases in the MII, the Network University Medicine (NUM) and other distributed research networks. We thank all site administrators, process developers, testers and members of the DSF and Operaton communities who contributed to this successful rollout.
